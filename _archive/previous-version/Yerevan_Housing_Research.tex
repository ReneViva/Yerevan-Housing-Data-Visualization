\subsection{Introduction}\label{introduction}

A lower advertised rent may leave more money for other needs. A longer
commute can consume some of that saving through transport spending and
time. This study asks whether those extra costs outweigh the saving for
comparable rental choices in Yerevan. The answer is allowed to be either
yes or no.

We study \textbf{advertisements from one agency}, not actual signed
leases or a random sample of households. The main comparison uses
two-room apartments, 45--75 m², advertised as furnished. Room and area
filters improve comparability; missing renovation and energy-efficiency
information prevents a quality-adjusted claim.

For each named district we construct an \textbf{example monthly budget}
from its sample-median asking rent, median round-trip road distance, and
median route duration. These medians may describe different apartments.
The result is a modeled district budget, not an observed household bill
or the median of summed apartment-level costs. Kentron is the
central-area comparator; its homes still have their own journeys to the
common destination, Republic Square.

\subsubsection{Research question and competing
hypotheses}\label{research-question-and-competing-hypotheses}

Does rent saved relative to Kentron exceed additional transport,
selected utilities, and optionally valued commuting time? For each named
comparison, define Delta as its modeled cost minus Kentron's. Negative
Delta means the comparison is cheaper under that scenario; positive
Delta means it is dearer. The competing possibilities are Delta
\textless{} 0, Delta = 0, and Delta \textgreater{} 0.

An inferential null would be H0: Delta = 0, versus H1: Delta is not
zero, with alpha = 0.05 fixed before results. \textbf{We do not run a
population hypothesis test here.} One agency's convenience catalogue,
uncertain dwelling independence, and constructed budgets do not justify
the intended citywide p-values or confidence intervals. This is a
descriptive and sensitivity study. A p-value, even if available, would
not establish causation or prove a hypothesis.

\subsubsection{Two outcomes and their
limits}\label{two-outcomes-and-their-limits}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Direct selected cost:} asking rent + modeled electricity, gas
  and water + modeled commuting spending, in AMD per household per
  30-day month.
\item
  \textbf{Generalized selected cost:} direct selected cost + modeled
  commuting hours multiplied by an assumed AMD/hour value of time.
\end{itemize}

These are selected housing-and-commuting components, not a complete
living-cost index. Food, internet, waste charges, building fees, moving
costs, deposits, health costs and full car ownership are outside the
measured model. Heating is included once through gas. Valued time is an
opportunity-cost scenario, not an extra cash payment or a verified loss
of earnings. A zero time value must give exactly the direct-cost
outcome.

All sources were accessed on 5 November 2026. Raw factual extracts,
hashes, retrieval metadata and route replies are retained separately
from processed tables. Only analytical housing fields were extracted;
contacts, images, owner names and full address numbers were excluded.
Source robots explicitly permits the public API, but that is not an
open-data license. Do not republish the catalogue as an openly licensed
dataset. The frozen 1 November agency district index is retained as
separate context and is \textbf{not pooled} into this later listing
sample.

The geography is \textbf{named agency-labeled Yerevan districts}.
Outside Kentron does not mean outside Yerevan. Abovyan and Masis are not
compared: adequate matched town samples and journeys were not
established. One Masis listing mislabeled as Kentron is excluded.
District labels and origin coordinates are not ground-truthed
administrative boundaries. No map is used to suggest precision we do not
have.

analysis chunks run; no result is supplied by invented example

\subsection{Statistical
interpretation}\label{statistical-interpretation}

No population p-values or confidence intervals are reported. The
advertised properties were not randomly sampled from Yerevan's rental
stock, source IDs do not establish independent dwellings, and most cost
inputs are modeled. Repeating a property across modes or time values
creates scenarios, not additional data. The boxplot IQR is observed
listing spread, not uncertainty about a population median. A bootstrap
of this agency catalogue would not remove its selection bias.

If a representative, independently sampled dataset became available, the
analysis would need an explicit estimand and sampling/clustering design
before selecting a test. A p-value would measure incompatibility with
that model's null, not the probability that the hypothesis is true. A
95\% confidence interval would describe a procedure's long-run coverage
under its assumptions, not a 95\% chance that a fixed parameter lies in
the computed interval. Statistical significance would not establish a
practically important or causal effect. Here, model differences and
sensitivity checks answer the defensible descriptive question.

\subsection{Conditional conclusions and
recommendations}\label{conditional-conclusions-and-recommendations}

The executed comparison at half the gross hourly benchmark is:

Private car: 10 of 10 non-Kentron district examples are cheaper than
Kentron. Public transport: 10 of 10 non-Kentron district examples are
cheaper than Kentron.

This applies to \textbf{one commuter, 22 commuting days, the stated
winter consumption and travel assumptions, and this agency's selected
advertisements}. It does not mean that the same number of districts is
cheaper for every renter or that any district is statistically proven
cheaper. Direct and generalized costs must be read separately; the
zero-time scenario remains the direct selected-cost outcome.

At the baseline settings, the lowest modeled generalized example is
Erebuni for public transport and Erebuni for private car. These are
conditional example rankings, not guaranteed available apartments or a
citywide optimal-location recommendation. Sparse districts deserve
particular caution.

The baseline model does not support the blanket claim that lower
noncentral rents are necessarily erased by the selected extra costs. In
the chosen household/heating/time grid, 2 noncentral rows reverse to a
higher cost than Kentron. This count describes the chosen grid, not the
likelihood that a household will face higher costs. The sensitivity
figure shows the specified two-commuter winter stress comparison.

A household can use the comparison by obtaining an actual rent quote,
checking the condition and heating system, timing its own outward and
return commute, and replacing our assumptions with its own fare/pass
choice, driving costs and time value. Lower rent is financially
advantageous when it exceeds the extra cash costs; it remains
advantageous in generalized terms only when it also compensates for the
household's value of additional time. The break-even table and
sensitivity chart show where that conditional answer can reverse.

\subsection{Limitations}\label{limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  One agency's active catalogue is a convenience snapshot. It may
  overrepresent certain prices, districts, building types or clients.
  Asking rent is not agreed rent; creation date is not a known
  price-update date, and active ads can have future or restricted
  availability.
\item
  Furniture is advertised, renovation is unstandardized, and building
  details are incomplete. Area differences remain inside the size band.
  Neither narrow filtering nor the robustness checks makes this a causal
  or quality-adjusted estimate.
\item
  District tags and coordinates may be approximate. Known placeholders,
  explicit town mismatches and ambiguous border cases were removed;
  remaining points were not ground-truthed. Public road routes use
  OpenStreetMap contributors' data, with cached OSRM model outputs, not
  live congestion or a transit timetable.
\item
  Winter consumption, access/waiting, congestion, marginal car costs and
  time values are assumptions. Full car ownership, shared-car commuting,
  actual taxi fares, summer/annual-average energy use and unsupported
  budget components are outside the model. Longer ticket commitments and
  eligible discounts can differ.
\item
  The 2025 national gross wage is a benchmark, not a 2026 personal net
  wage. Heating stress is not a measured geographic effect. No universal
  distance threshold, municipality-external comparison, or population
  significance claim is supported.
\item
  Changing the aggregation to medians of listing-level modeled totals
  changed 0 comparison signs; this is one limited robustness check, not
  complete immunity to bias or changed assumptions.
\end{itemize}
